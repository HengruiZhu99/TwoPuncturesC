\noindent\textbf{Current conclusion.} The selectable trumpet seed, coupled solver
interface, portable checkpoint, and AthenaK consumer are implemented. Independent
seed checks and selected horizon measurements pass. \textbf{No complete binary
physical-validation claim is supported yet.} The moderate three-grid sequence
fails its convergence requirement; both extreme binaries exceed the declared
physical-constraint tolerances. The reduced-factor radial-512 boost retry has
completed and improves the constraint errors, but still fails their bounds.

\section{Construction and its limits}
The reference is the $R_0=M$ stationary Kerr trumpet of Dennison, Baumgarte and
Montero, Eq.~12 of arXiv:1409.1887v2. The reused AthenaK reference is pinned to
\path{project/kerr-trumpet-spin09}, commit
\path{81c7d8030c49489577c54b977b8b7f7b42d9a96e}.
This is distinct from both Ian Ruchlin's quasi-isotropic wormhole HiSpID free data
and a stationary standard $1+\log$ trumpet. The existing quasi-isotropic family
remains the default; residual-Z4c evolution background subtraction is not added
to the elliptic equations.

We use signature $(-+++)$ and $K_{ij}=-\mathcal L_n\gamma_{ij}/2$.
For rest mass $m$, $a=|\boldsymbol S|/m$, and $R=r+m$, one scalar choice is
$\psi_0=(\Sigma/r^2)^{1/4}$ with
$\Sigma=R^2+a^2\cos^2\theta$; the other is $(\det\gamma)^{1/12}$.
Both have direction-dependent $r^{-1/2}$ puncture behavior. Full spacetime
Lorentz pullbacks supply boosted spatial tensors and their time derivatives.
A rest-chart connection contraction gives the extrinsic curvature stably.
Spacelikeness and positive spatial metrics are checked without clipping.
Nonspinning boosted slicing has an analytic global spacelikeness argument;
general spinning cases retain sampled-domain checks.

The four elliptic unknowns are a scalar correction $u$ and vector $b^i$.
Outside the explicitly modified interior, the CTT equations are
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde\Delta\psi-\tfrac18\widetilde R\psi
 -\tfrac1{12}K^2\psi^5+\tfrac18\widetilde A_{ij}\widetilde A^{ij}\psi^{-7}&=0,\\
 \widetilde D_j\widetilde A^{ij}-\tfrac23\psi^6\widetilde D^iK&=0,
 \qquad \widetilde A^{ij}=\widetilde A_0^{ij}+(\widetilde Lb)^{ij}.
\end{align*}
The covariant CTT tensor is $\widetilde A_{ij}=\psi^2(K_{ij}-\gamma_{ij}K/3)$;
AthenaK's Z4c conformal tensor instead uses $\psi^{-4}$ on the same physical
trace-free tensor. The import explicitly converts these conventions.

Unmodified trumpet corrections cannot simply be assumed analytic at the
puncture: the isolated Schwarzschild scalar indicial exponent includes
$p_0=\sqrt3-1/2$. The implemented binary examples use the declared interior
plateau, where the correction equations become
$\Delta u=0$ and $\Delta b+\nabla\operatorname{div}b/3=0$.
Bounded corrections then have removable singularities. This modifies the
interior operator; vacuum accuracy is tested independently outside it, and
measured horizons must enclose the modified regions. It is not evidence of
exponential convergence across the transition.

\section{Acceptance and production path}
The production stages are host extended-precision geometry/cache construction,
CUDA GMRES for the solve, CPU sampling, and OpenMP AthenaK horizon integration.
The opt-in no-swirl axisymmetric Krylov projection is restricted to nonspinning
axial configurations; full unprojected nonlinear residuals still govern acceptance.
Default QI and Bowen--York paths are preserved. No backend benchmark matrix is
part of this investigation.

12. The head-on target has opposite seed $\Gamma=10$, masses $0.5$, and
separation 25. The latter has not yet been calibrated with qualified binary
irreducible masses.

The moderate sequence fails despite two grids satisfying pointwise bounds:
bulk errors do not decrease. Tighter nonlinear stopping and halved observer
steps did not resolve this. A targeted replay localizes most of the finest-grid
bulk Hamiltonian error to the scalar $m=6$ contribution: removing just its
cosine/sine pair reduces the RMS from $2.62\times10^{-7}$ to
$5.20\times10^{-9}$, while momentum is unchanged. The unmodified replay matches
the saved result exactly. This is a sensitivity experiment, not an accepted
filtered solution or proof that the true mode vanishes; its axis regularity
and numerical conditioning need investigation. The retained $m=6$ polynomial
also has a forbidden $\rho^4$ axis term: its largest sampled coefficient grows
from $4.89\times10^{-8}$ to $8.92\times10^{-7}$ across the sequence.
An opt-in C4 basis, with exact powers through $m=6$, has passed local
manufactured-operator and metadata checks. Its CUDA control also passes
(maximum normalized error $8.78\times10^{-13}$), but the moderate binary
trial failed its second Newton line search after 31 Krylov iterations.
A targeted retry from its retained iterate with linear forcing $0.001$
instead of $0.1$ also fails, despite achieving a true linear residual ratio
$9.08\times10^{-4}$. Free data, grid and native image are identical. A one-step
trace reproduces failure with positive $\psi$, finite residuals and normalized
linear descent slope $-0.999999$. Its nonlinear residual remains $1.073$ times
the initial value at the final allowed damping $1/1024$. The modal-step maximum
is $3.35\times10^{12}$ and the small-step Taylor remainder decreases with damping.
An isolated build permitting 24 halvings accepts a step at $1/2048$, but its
next linear solve exhausts 2400 iterations at relative residual $0.0346$
(target $0.001$). The default remains 10 and acceptance bounds are unchanged.
Two preconditioner-only scaling prototypes also fail a small convergence
control and are rejected. The original C4 solver converges on that small grid
in 6 Newton/135 Krylov steps. A CUDA coarse-to-fine continuation also fails:
the coarse $12\times24\times16$ solve converges in 6 Newton/133 Krylov
steps, but the $192\times384\times16$ solve stops after 2 Newton/2526 Krylov
steps (252.7 s solve, 429.4 s workflow). Its second linear solve reaches only
relative residual $0.0181$ against $0.001$. The coarse solution already fails
independent physical bounds; the final fine iterate is substantially worse
(near-hole $H/M$ RMS $0.559/0.155$, bulk $7.35/3.50$). Algebraic coarse
convergence therefore does not supply a successful fine-grid initial guess.
A no-solve comparison of the coarse and prolonged fields at 102 observer
points gives maximum scaled field difference $1.64\times10^{-13}$ and
metric-gradient difference $3.19\times10^{-12}$. A gross transfer defect at
these points is therefore excluded. Decomposing the accepted step identifies
$m=6$ as the largest sampled perturbation ($\max|\Delta K_{ij}|=0.505$,
$\max|\Delta\partial\gamma|=0.572$), motivating investigation of high-mode
conditioning. No modes are removed from an accepted solution.
A scalar-source probe on a Perlmutter CPU provides evidence of an angular
roundoff floor. At $x=2.7$, $\rho=10^{-3}$, the cached $m=6$ Fourier
amplitude is $2.68\times10^{-16}$; recombining geometry in extended precision
gives $1.44\times10^{-18}$. At $x=3.5$, $\rho=0.003$, the corresponding
values are $6.44\times10^{-17}$ and $2.32\times10^{-19}$. The source should
have sixth-order radial decay where smooth; both evaluations eventually
reach a precision floor. These components can be amplified by the inverse
axis factor in the C4 preconditioner. This scalar-only diagnostic does not
establish the cause of all coupled-system errors or qualify a remedy.
An opt-in source rearrangement now combines the background Hamiltonian
source before narrowing and evaluates correction power differences in
factored form. Default behavior is unchanged. The small nonlinear control
converges in 6 Newton/129 Krylov iterations, and its directional derivative
check reaches relative error $1.00\times10^{-11}$. Converged sampled fields
differ from the original small-grid result by $1.03\times10^{-7}$ in scaled
maximum norm, so strict field equivalence is not claimed. The bounded GPU replay completes in 42 Krylov iterations (14.7 s solve),
but the modal-step maximum remains $1.63\times10^{12}$ and the first accepted
damping is $1/2048$, with residual ratio $0.999575$. This does not cure the
large-update behavior or justify a full solve. The option remains experimental
and has not been qualified for production.
A separate preconditioner audit finds negative centered-stencil neighbor
weights near the axis: the interior cell ratio $|b|h/(2a)$ reaches $4.33$ for
regularity exponent six. An opt-in monotone variant uses
$a_{\rm prec}=\max(a,|b|h/2)$ in the approximate inverse only. Residual and
JVP witnesses remain bitwise identical. Its small nonlinear control takes
5 Newton/98 Krylov iterations versus 6/135 previously. The fine-grid replay
takes 59 Krylov iterations and 16.4 s solve time, but retains a modal-step
maximum $1.43\times10^{12}$. Its first accepted damping is $1/1024$, with
residual ratio $0.999441$. It therefore does not resolve the large-update
problem and has no production or physical-accuracy qualification.
A standalone scalar experiment instead imposes endpoint regularity on C2
modal variables, replacing near-axis collocation rows by independent
endpoint constraints. For an analytic $m=6$ field, errors decrease from
$5.82\times10^{-6}$ to $2.93\times10^{-9}$ to $2.40\times10^{-14}$ on
$12\times24$, $20\times40$, and $32\times64$ grids. An independent Cartesian
Hessian verifies the forcing to $7.81\times10^{-17}$ in scaled maximum norm.
An absolute $10^{-16}$ source perturbation gives finest-grid field noise
$2.17\times10^{-11}$, versus $3.43\times10^{-6}$ with factored C4 variables.
This motivates a native tau prototype; curved/vector/nonlinear and GPU
qualification remain outstanding. The native opt-in tau path now implements
these endpoint equations in CPU and Kokkos residuals/JVPs, with matching
approximate-inverse boundary rows and a distinct checkpoint identifier.
Its small coupled solve converges in 5 Newton/69 Krylov steps. Endpoint-row
reconstruction agrees to $1.11\times10^{-16}$ and the directional derivative
check to $9.74\times10^{-12}$. The CUDA structural check also passes (endpoint-row error
$3.33\times10^{-16}$, directional derivative error $1.00\times10^{-11}$ and
exact checkpoint roundtrip). The moderate fine-grid attempt exhausts 2400 Krylov iterations at relative
residual $0.00895$ (target $0.001$), without a Newton update. Its independent
physical constraints fail. Exact radial endpoint rows now replace the local
approximation in the preconditioner at unchanged matrix storage. The small
solve uses 61 rather than 69 Krylov iterations, with sampled fields agreeing
to $1.01\times10^{-13}$. However, the fine-grid replay again exhausts 2400
iterations, now at relative residual $0.0162$, so radial rows alone are
insufficient. An opt-in rank-$2n_A$ Woodbury correction now enforces exact
polar endpoint rows while retaining the block-tridiagonal base factors.
Its response columns are built with batched triangular solves. An independent
dense-matrix control, including nontrivial pivots and inhomogeneous endpoint
data, gives solution error below $6.7\times10^{-15}$. The small coupled CPU
solve uses 53 Krylov iterations, preserves the residual/JVP witnesses bitwise,
and agrees in sampled fields/gradients to $2.53\times10^{-13}$. Host/device
allocation bounds include the additional response and Schur matrices.
The CUDA small control also passes (5 Newton/54 Krylov, field/gradient
agreement $2.82\times10^{-13}$). Shared-GPU job 59420201 has proceeded to the
fine-grid linear replay; its result is pending. No new horizon or binary
acceptance is claimed.
No conclusion
about converged C4 binary accuracy is available. It has a distinct basis identifier and has not
replaced the C2 production default. The retained boost map is $(\lambda,\kappa)=(0.03,3.5)$.
Radial refinement from 160 to 256 improved its errors by factors 2--6.
Stronger focusing, $\lambda=0.003$, worsened both independent errors and radial
tails and is rejected. Radial-512 mapped-operator controls pass at normalized
error $5.49\times10^{-10}$. After the original timeout, the reduced-factor
retry completed: relative to radial 256, near-hole Hamiltonian/momentum RMS
errors improve by factors $8.09/3.95$ and bulk errors by $4.18/3.39$.
Extrapolated energy changes by $5.48\times10^{-6}$ relatively. These improvements
do not meet the physical bounds; the diagnostic horizon check also fails.

\section{Horizon measurements and computational cost}
The finest moderate checkpoint passes import and horizon angular/quadrature
checks, but remains diagnostic because of its constraint sequence. Its component
irreducible masses are $0.5887780114$ and $0.3935773863$.

The spin-target diagnostic checkpoint passes orders $12,16,20$ and an independent
quadrature increase. Each component has
$M_H=0.500017851900$, $M_{\rm irr}=0.377763585476$, and coordinate
$\chi=0.989929520988$, with expansion RMS $5.98\times10^{-9}$.
Both retained surfaces enclose the modified regions. These are coordinate
rotation-integral spins, not approximate-Killing-vector estimates.
Reusing passed harmonic shapes strictly as initial guesses reduces order-12/16
runs from $671/1227$ seconds to $35.5/50.4$ seconds; all geometry and acceptance
checks are recomputed.

For boosted horizons an optional spatial chart
$x=c+J(y-c)$, $J=I-vv^T/(1+\sqrt{1-v^2})$, makes the exact isolated seed surface
spherical. Tensors and derivatives are pulled back consistently; physical
rotation generators become $J^{-1}E_iJ$. This changes no slice.
The isolated $\Gamma=10$ control passes orders $4,8,12$: relative area error
$\le7.22\times10^{-11}$, shape error $\le1.13\times10^{-10}$, and expansion
RMS $\le4.78\times10^{-11}$. The solved binary instead stalls near
$1.8\times10^{-5}$ at both orders 8 and 16; it has no qualified horizon result.
At radial 512 the order-8 expansion floor falls to $9.49\times10^{-6}$ after
1000 iterations, still above $10^{-7}$. Import succeeds with ADM/Z4c relative
error $4.39\times10^{-16}$. The driver stops at the failed surface; its area
does not supply a qualified irreducible mass for separation calibration.

Necessary production solves, not a matched-accuracy benchmark, took
94.2 seconds (315 seconds total, 4.87 GiB peak process RSS) for the listed spin
case and 161.3 seconds (273 seconds total, 3.53 GiB RSS) for the listed boost
case. Host RSS excludes GPU allocation. The radial-512 run retains the existing
64 GiB aggregate guard; its conservative estimate is 63.74 GiB.
The completed radial-512 retry took 3119.6 seconds in the nonlinear solve
(3294.6 seconds total, 5.59 GiB peak host RSS), with 10 Newton steps and
352 Krylov iterations. It used the original column-wise transfer construction.

The longer radial-512 attempt exposed a host factor-construction bottleneck:
a live stack snapshot found active threads in the modal preconditioner's
column-wise triangular solves. The qualified opt-in implementation constructs
$A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(U)$ with two matrix-right-hand-side triangular solves,
retaining the same LU factors, pivots and block recurrence. On a 512-row
pivoted control, the original-matrix residual is $7.41\times10^{-16}$ and the
old/new transfer difference is $5.11\times10^{-16}$. Necessary kernel timings
on Perlmutter are $0.556$ versus $0.107$ seconds (a factor of $5.19$);
\textbf{this is not a whole-solver speedup measurement}. A local small-solve
field/gradient comparison agrees within $1.31\times10^{-16}$, and the separate
CUDA axisymmetric control passes. The option remains off by default. A
continuation command pins the tested image and preserves the radial-512
configuration. It was not launched because the original run completed;
future necessary solves can use the qualified optimization.
Evidence is in \path{validation/trumpet/batched-transfer/} and
\path{validation/trumpet/axial-factors/stack-snapshot.txt}.

validation. This report is an interim snapshot, not completion of the goal.
